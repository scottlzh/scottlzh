The lab welcomes inquiries from prospective Ph.D. students, master's students, undergraduate researchers, visiting students, and collaborators interested in AI-enabled transportation systems, transportation resilience, connected and automated vehicles, traffic flow modeling, and active traffic safety.

## Prospective Graduate Students

Applicants with backgrounds in transportation engineering, civil engineering, computer science, data science, control, operations research, statistics, or related areas are encouraged to reach out. Useful preparation includes programming experience, mathematical modeling, machine learning, simulation, optimization, or transportation systems analysis.

Availability depends on research fit, timing, and funding. In an initial email, please include a short research-interest statement, CV, transcript if applicable, and links to publications, code, posters, or prior projects.

## Undergraduate Researchers and Visitors

Undergraduate researchers and visiting students can contribute to projects involving data analysis, simulation, literature synthesis, visualization, benchmarking, and reproducible research tools. Please describe your technical background, expected timeline, and topics of interest when contacting the lab.

## Collaboration

The group is open to collaborations on resilient transportation infrastructure, freight and port resilience, cyber-physical safety of automated vehicles, multimodal AI benchmarks, and decision-support systems for mobility operations.
